# 10. Relic Visual Design Canon

## 10.1 Historical / material boundary

All relic imagery is fully analog. The latest acceptable visual language is roughly late 18th to 19th century; older medieval, folkloric, archival and ancient imagery is welcome where appropriate. Act IV boss/event material may of course be substantially older and Egyptian-inspired.

**Never use:** computers, screens, typewriters, photocopiers, modern ring binders, plastic, ballpoint pens, modern office staplers, self-inking rubber stamps, barcodes, QR codes, machine-readable punch cards, fluorescent office supplies, modern cash registers, contemporary filing cabinets, digital clocks, electronic displays, modern corporate logos, or other modern-office shorthand.

Administrative objects should instead be built from:

- parchment, rag paper, vellum, papyrus;
- wood, horn, bone, ivory-like material;
- iron, pewter, brass, copper, bronze, silver, gold;
- wax, twine, ribbon, linen, leather;
- quills, reeds, chalk, stone, clay, glass;
- hand-written marks, manual punches, carved tallies, seals and bindings.

## 10.2 Drawing language

1. **Silhouette first.** At small icon size, two relics from the same family should remain distinguishable as black silhouettes whenever reasonably possible.
2. **One signature twist per relic.** The noun in the title establishes the base object; the visual brief below adds one strong, unique feature that prevents repeated stamps, rings, papers, weights and books from collapsing into the same icon.
3. **Simple enough to draw.** Avoid tiny ornament fields, photorealistic texture, complex perspective or crowded scenery. One object or one compact object-group is preferred.
4. **Black outline remains primary.** Use slightly imperfect hand-inked linework, not vector-perfect geometry.
5. **Maximum five visible colors per relic, including frame/background colors.** Subtle shades of the same hue count as one family and should not become gradient-heavy.
6. **No animation requirement.** Every specification is for one static icon/sprite.
7. **No characters unless absolutely unavoidable.** Relics should normally be represented by objects, fragments, instruments or symbols rather than people.
8. **No text as the primary identity.** The title and catalogue number appear below the icon; internal object text should be minimal, hand-written, and never required to understand the silhouette.

## 10.3 Catalogue label rule

Every generated relic must show the exact canonical catalogue label beneath the icon:

> **`<number>. <exact relic title>`**

Use an old serif or hand-lettered archival face. Never alter spelling, punctuation, apostrophes or hyphenation. The number and title belong **below** the object, centered. No rarity label is required on the icon itself.

## 10.4 Pool frames and color identity

### Normal Relics — #1–50
- **Frame:** thin **gray** frame with clipped corners, restrained and practical.
- **Color role:** mostly parchment, charcoal and gray with one or two muted object accents.
- **Visual hierarchy:** object first; frame quiet.

### Shop Relics — #51–74
- **Frame:** clearly recognizable **copper** frame, slightly heavier than Normal, with tiny coin-notch or hand-hammered details.
- **Color role:** copper is always present; optional verdigris should remain subtle.
- **Visual hierarchy:** visibly commercial / valuable without becoming gaudy.

### Event Relics — #75–99
- **Frame:** **pale violet** frame, slightly irregular and uncanny, with one small source/event flourish.
- **Color role:** pale violet is the pool identifier; the object may use one or two additional muted accents.
- **Visual hierarchy:** strange, found, singular; never generic loot.

### Boss Relics — #100–168
- **Frame:** **epic dark-purple and antique-gold frame** with an ivory inner window and boss-family crest.
- **Color role:** dark purple + antique gold are mandatory; one object-specific accent may be added.
- **Visual hierarchy:** the frame should make a Boss Relic recognizable before the object is read, but the object silhouette must remain clear.

## 10.5 Color budget

The palette line in every relic entry is a **maximum palette**, not a requirement to fill every color. Avoid gradients. Prefer flat muted fills, sparse hatching and paper texture.

## 10.6 Family taxonomy

The family label is a production tool used to audit silhouette collisions. It does **not** change mechanics.

- **Stamps & Seals**
- **Rings & Signets**
- **Documents, Writs & Tickets**
- **Books & Archive**
- **Tokens, Coins & Tallies**
- **Threads, Ribbons & Textiles**
- **Tools & Implements**
- **Vessels & Containers**
- **Measures, Weights & Instruments**
- **Keys, Locks & Access**
- **Ritual & Natural Charms**
- **Time & Delay**
- **Monuments, Stones & Architecture**

**Collision rule:** before approving a relic, compare it against every previously approved relic in the same family. If the silhouette and central feature are too similar, preserve the canonical title but exaggerate the listed signature feature until the icons are distinguishable.

# 12. Production Workflow for 20-Relic Image Batches

When generating sprite sheets or concept sheets from this canon:

1. Take the next **20 catalogue numbers in numerical order**.
2. Copy their titles **verbatim** from this file.
3. Copy each relic's family, frame, palette, object/silhouette and signature cue into the image-generation brief.
4. State explicitly: **do not invent, rename, substitute or reorder any relic**.
5. Use the pool frame exactly as specified. Mixed-pool sheets are allowed; each individual relic keeps its own frame.
6. Keep each object centered with generous whitespace.
7. Put the exact `number. title` **beneath** each icon.
8. After generation, audit every cell against this document before counting the batch as finished.
9. If even one title or object has drifted, regenerate or replace that cell; do not advance the completion counter until the batch is correct.
10. For repeated families (especially stamps, seals, rings, documents, weights, books and tokens), check the signature differentiation cue at thumbnail size.

## 12.1 Recommended sheet order

- Sheet 1: 1–20
- Sheet 2: 21–40
- Sheet 3: 41–60
- Sheet 4: 61–80
- Sheet 5: 81–100
- Sheet 6: 101–120
- Sheet 7: 121–140
- Sheet 8: 141–160
- Sheet 9: 161–168

## 12.2 Final approval test

A relic icon is approved only if all are true:

- exact canonical number;
- exact canonical title;
- correct pool frame;
- maximum five-color rule respected;
- analog / historical material language respected;
- family is visually identifiable;
- signature cue is visible;
- icon is not confusingly similar to another relic in the same family;
- no modern office object or digital element has slipped in;
- the icon remains readable at small game-UI size.
