## 3. Final Naming Rule

Boss names may be longer and more ceremonial than standard-enemy names, but should read as **titles and epithets**, not explanatory sentences. Act-IV titles were therefore tightened to forms such as `The Pharaoh of the Sealed Name`, `The Vizier of the King's Mouth` and `The Queen of the Flood Reckoning`.

Folkloric names may still be uncanny and poetic without becoming full clauses; `The Answering Hill` preserves the same fairy-tale identity in a compact title. Act V keeps its parallel divine `Name – Epithet` structure because the repetition there is liturgical and deliberate rather than generative.

---

## 4. Cross-Act Boss Philosophy

A boss should not be a standard enemy with more HP and a stronger attack table.

A boss must create its own **rule world**.

Bosses may use:

- phases;
- source-bound resources;
- altered action meaning;
- conditional vulnerability;
- visible future actions;
- stored past actions;
- card-instance custody;
- rule publication;
- player-authored constraints;
- environmental state;
- institutional or divine UI areas.

But every boss must remain:

- readable after learning;
- explicitly telegraphed;
- strategically answerable;
- fair across legitimate archetypes;
- bounded against loops and softlocks;
- explicit about transition timing and cleanup.

The intended escalation is:

> **Act I:** bureaucracy becomes governing systems.  
> **Act II:** archival logic becomes governing structures.  
> **Act III:** remembered custom becomes jurisdiction and sovereign law.  
> **Act IV:** sacred administration becomes the institutions of an ancient state.  
> **Act V:** administration is revealed as cosmic order itself.

## 1. Final Audit Verdict

Act I uses a final pool of **5 bosses**:

1. **The Deputy Undersecretary**
2. **The Queue Commissioner**
3. **The Lord Sealkeeper**
4. **The Municipal Dragon**
5. **The Living Charter**

All five character identities are retained.

The old shared Act-I boss structure of:

> approximately 50% HP → generic Phase II

is removed.

Each boss now transitions according to its **own mechanical narrative**.

The final bosses follow this hierarchy:

> **Standard enemies teach recognizable combat rules.**  
> **Elite encounters force the player to negotiate with a system.**  
> **Bosses create an entire rule world.**

Each final boss therefore has a distinct combat contract.

| Boss | Mechanical thesis |
|---|---|
| The Deputy Undersecretary | You cannot finish everything. Decide what may officially remain unresolved. |
| The Queue Commissioner | You are inside the queue and must manipulate when and how you are served. |
| The Lord Sealkeeper | Dismantle his authority and choose which protection disappears first. |
| The Municipal Dragon | Steal civic authority from the hoard and use it against an increasingly illegal dragon. |
| The Living Charter | Combat rules are law; law can be reviewed, amended and interpreted. |

No boss is intended to function as:

- a larger elite;
- a pure HP sponge;
- a single repeated damage check;
- a generic two-phase damage escalation;
- or a blanket archetype counter.

# 2. Act-I Boss Balance Frame

The finalized Act-I standard-combat pool establishes approximately:

- solo standard enemies up to **69 HP**;
- ordinary standard attacks around **5–12 damage**;
- large telegraphed standard attacks around **12–18 damage**;
- standard Block values around **6–18**;
- late standard multi-enemy encounters approaching roughly **88 combined HP**.

Bosses therefore receive only a moderate raw-stat increase.

# 3. Shared Act-I Boss Rules

These rules are binding unless a boss explicitly overrides them.

## 3.1 Boss-specific transitions

There is no shared 50%-HP phase rule.

Each boss transitions because its own encounter state demands it.

HP thresholds may exist only as:

- a secondary transition condition;
- an anti-stall failsafe;
- or a thematic trigger specific to that boss.

## 3.2 Transition timing

A phase transition never interrupts the middle of a player action or unresolved damage event.

When a transition condition becomes true:

> the transition replaces the boss's next legal normal intent.

The transition is visibly previewed.

## 3.3 Telegraphing

Major boss consequences must always provide a readable response window.

Examples:

- Executive-file escalation;
- Service Windows;
- Reclaim the Seal;
- Municipal Inferno;
- Judicial Review;
- Constitutional Crisis effects.

No boss signature may appear as an invisible trigger surprise.

## 3.4 Persistent boss-state limit

Encounter UI should display no more than roughly **three major persistent boss-state systems** at once.

Examples:

- Deputy: Matters + Backlog / Executive Files
- Commissioner: Queue position + Priority + Service state
- Sealkeeper: Great Seals / Fragments
- Dragon: Permits + Authorization + Code Violations
- Charter: Active Articles + interpretation + Exception

Individual intents and ordinary combat statuses do not count as separate persistent boss systems.

## 3.5 Deck remains the primary solution space

Boss systems modify the value of normal deck actions.

They must not replace the deckbuilder with an unrelated minigame.

Damage, Block, card sequencing, status use, resource management and target timing remain the player's main tools.

## 3.6 No recursive boss-rule loops

Boss-specific triggered effects may not recursively generate themselves.

Examples:

- Mutual Security Block does not trigger Mutual Security again.
- Redress Block does not create Redress.
- pending status activation under Due Notice is not a second status application.
- Authorization does not generate Authorization.
- Broken Seal Fragment use does not generate new Fragments.

## 3.7 Full cleanup

At boss death or combat end, all boss-local resources are removed.

No boss-state object persists into the next combat.

# 4. The Deputy Undersecretary

**HP:** **130**  
**Role:** workload management / accumulated consequences  
**Difficulty target:** Medium–High Act-I boss  
**Mechanical thesis:**

> **You cannot finish everything. Decide what may officially remain unresolved.**

## 4.1 Character / visual identity

The Deputy Undersecretary is a visibly senior human administrator whose body and office have begun to merge.

The desk is not scenery. It is part of the encounter.

Suggested visual direction:

- robes layered with docket ribbons and seals;
- an immense desk with sliding trays;
- unanswered files physically piling around the body;
- writing implements moving independently;
- the office becoming increasingly congested as Backlog rises.

The boss should remain recognizably human and institutional.

It must not become a cosmic archive entity; that conceptual space belongs to later Acts.

# Phase I — Routine Administration

## 4.2 The Desk

The Desk holds at most:

> **3 active Matters**

Each Matter occupies a visible slot.

A Matter begins with:

> **Due 2**

At the end of each player turn:

> unresolved Matter → Due −1.

At Due 0:

1. resolve its Overdue consequence;
2. add **1 Backlog**;
3. remove the Matter from the Desk.

### Backlog

Maximum:

> **5 Backlog**

If a new Backlog would be created while already at 5:

> Backlog remains 5 and the Matter resolves only its explicit Overdue consequence.

No further Backlog scaling occurs.

## 4.3 Matter eligibility

Each generated Matter must be individually possible from the current deterministic state.

The boss may never require:

- unknown future draws;
- unavailable card types;
- impossible Energy expenditure;
- an unavailable status-removal effect;
- or an action the current hand cannot theoretically perform.

However:

> multiple simultaneous Matters do **not** need to be jointly solvable.

That tension is intentional.

The player may be forced to choose which Matter becomes overdue.

## 4.4 Matter categories

There are three categories:

- **Performance**
- **Expenditure**
- **Procedural**

These categories are remembered when Backlog is created and later determine Phase-II Executive Files.

### Complaint of Insufficient Action — Performance

**Resolve:** Deal at least **12 HP damage** to the Deputy during this player turn.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Performance Backlog.

### Petition for Immediate Relief — Performance

**Resolve:** Gain at least **10 total Block** during this player turn.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Performance Backlog.

### Request for Additional Review — Expenditure

**Resolve:** Pay **1 Energy** using the Matter action.

This payment is a separate boss-context action. It is not a card.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Expenditure Backlog.

### Application Fee Outstanding — Expenditure

**Resolve:** Pay **1 Energy** using the Matter action, then play at least one non-Junk card before ending the turn.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Expenditure Backlog.

### Notice of Missing Response — Procedural

Eligible only when the current hand contains at least one playable Attack and one playable Skill.

**Resolve:** Play at least one Attack and one Skill during this turn.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Procedural Backlog.

### Request for Clean Record — Procedural

Eligible only when the current state allows the player to complete the condition meaningfully.

**Resolve:** End the turn without creating a new self-inflicted negative status or other explicitly avoidable player-side penalty.

**Overdue:** Gain 1 Procedural Backlog.

This Matter should not punish unavoidable boss-applied statuses during the same turn.

## 4.5 Phase-I intents

### Adjourn the Matter
Deal **9 damage**.  
Create one eligible Matter for the next player turn if Desk capacity allows.

### Circulate the Memorandum
Gain **14 Block**.  
Advance the oldest unresolved Matter by **1 Due** toward Overdue.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

### Come Back Later
Deal **7 damage**.  
Apply **1 Fatigue**.

### Request Further Particulars
Deal **8 damage**.  
Apply **1 Doubt**.  
If fewer than two Matters exist, create one eligible Matter.

### Deputy's Decision
Deal **14 damage**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

### Desk-full rule

If an intent would create a Matter while all three Desk slots are occupied:

> no fourth Matter is created.

Instead:

> Deputy gains **6 Block**.

A full Desk never creates instant Overdue.

# Transition — The Desk Is Full

Primary trigger:

> **Backlog reaches 4**

Failsafe trigger:

> Deputy reaches **58 HP or less**

The next boss action becomes:

## Declare the Matter Urgent

Gain **12 Block**.

Then resolve all currently open Matters:

- Due 1 Matters become Overdue and create their category Backlog;
- Due 2 Matters are closed without creating Backlog.

Backlog remains capped at 5.

Then Phase II begins.

# Phase II — Executive Disposition

Existing Backlog categories are converted into at most **three Executive Files**.

Each File may have:

> **Intensity 1–2**

Additional matching Backlog increases that File's intensity rather than adding more UI objects.

### Unanswered Complaint

Created from Performance Backlog.

At boss-turn start:

> Deputy gains **4 Block per intensity**

Maximum: **8 Block**

### Accumulated Delay

Created from Expenditure Backlog.

Every second player turn:

> Apply **1 Fatigue**

At Intensity 2 additionally:

> Apply **1 Doubt**

Never apply two Fatigue from this File in the same trigger.

### Defective Filing

Created from Procedural Backlog.

The first time each player turn the player receives a new negative status:

> Deputy gains **4 Block per intensity**

Maximum once per player turn.

## 4.6 Phase-II intents

### Executive Adjournment
Deal **11 damage**.  
Apply **1 Fatigue**.

### Disposition Memorandum
Deal **9 damage**.  
Gain **12 Block**.

### Decide Without Hearing
Deal **16 damage**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

### Close an Unanswered File
Consume **1 Executive File intensity**.  
Gain **1 Strength + 10 Block**.  
Maximum use: **2 times per combat**.  
If no Executive File intensity remains, this intent is ineligible.

## Signature — Everything Outstanding

Earliest after 3 ordinary boss actions in Phase II.

Deal:

> **14 + 2 per total remaining Executive File intensity**

Maximum:

> **20 damage**

Cooldown:

> **3 boss intents**

The attack does not consume Executive Files.

## 4.7 Counterplay

Phase I asks which Matters are worth resolving.

Phase II asks which consequences the player allowed to survive into executive disposition.

Different decks solve different Matter types naturally.

No single Matter category is mandatory.

# 5. The Queue Commissioner

**HP:** **126**  
**Role:** queue-position manipulation / scheduled vulnerability  
**Difficulty target:** Medium–High Act-I boss  
**Mechanical thesis:**

> **You are inside the queue.**

## 5.1 Character / visual identity

The Queue Commissioner is the final authority behind the early-Act queue bureaucracy.

Visual identity may include:

- numbered ribbons and brass calling bells;
- counters extending impossibly into the distance;
- numbered paper spirits waiting behind the player;
- a ceremonial queue staff;
- signs rearranging themselves as positions change.

This boss intentionally recurs from early Act-I queue language.

That recurrence is thematic escalation, not duplication.

# Phase I — The Living Queue

## 5.2 Queue Track

Visible track:

> **COUNTER ← 1 ← 2 ← 3 ← 4**

The player begins at:

> **Position 3**

At the end of each Commissioner turn:

> advance the player one position toward the Counter

unless that boss intent explicitly closes the Counter.

## 5.3 Administrative Choice

At the beginning of each normal player turn outside a Service Window, the player may choose exactly one:

### HOLD POSITION
No effect.

### PETITION FOR PRIORITY
Apply **1 Paperwork**.  
Move **1 position toward the Counter**.

### YIELD YOUR PLACE
Move **1 position away from the Counter**, maximum Position 4.  
Gain **6 Block**.

Only one administrative choice may be made per player turn.

## 5.4 Priority

The first time each player turn the Commissioner suffers at least:

> **14 player-caused HP loss**

the player gains:

> **Priority**

Maximum: **1**

Priority prevents the next boss-caused backward Queue movement.

Then Priority is consumed.

Priority does not stop normal Queue advancement.

## 5.5 Service Window

When the player reaches the Counter:

> the next player turn is **SERVING YOU**

At Service Window start:

> remove up to **10 Block** from the Commissioner.

During the Service Window:

> Commissioner takes **25% increased player-caused HP damage**.

Outside Service:

> Commissioner takes normal damage.

He is never immune or highly resistant outside the Window.

After the player turn:

> Service ends  
> player returns to **Position 3**

## 5.6 Phase-I intents

### Next, Please
Deal **9 damage**.  
Normal Queue advance occurs afterward.

### Reorder the Line
Deal **8 damage**.  
Apply **1 Panic**.  
Move player **1 position backward** unless Priority prevents it.

### Counter Closed
Gain **15 Block**.  
No normal Queue advance this round.  
Cooldown: **3 boss intents**.  
Cannot occur consecutively.

### Everyone Has Been Waiting
Deal **8 + 2 per Queue position beyond Position 1**.

Examples:

- Position 1 → 8
- Position 2 → 10
- Position 3 → 12
- Position 4 → 14

Maximum: **14 damage**

### Call a Number Not Yours
Deal **11 damage**.  
Apply **1 Doubt**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

# Transition — Counter of Final Appeal

Trigger:

> after the second completed Service Window

or failsafe:

> Commissioner reaches **60 HP or less**

The next boss action becomes:

## Open the Final Counter

Deal **7 damage**.  
Gain **10 Block**.

Queue becomes:

> **COUNTER ← 1 ← 2 ← 3**

Player moves to:

> **Position 2**

Phase II begins.

# Phase II — Priority Queue

Service Windows now offer one choice.

## STANDARD SERVICE

For this player turn:

> Commissioner takes **30% increased player-caused HP damage**.

After the turn:

> player gains **1 Paperwork**  
> player returns to **Position 2**

## EXPEDITED SERVICE

For this player turn:

> Commissioner takes **15% increased player-caused HP damage**.

After the turn:

> player moves to **Position 1**

The player trades immediate burst quality for faster access to the next Service Window.

## 5.7 Phase-II intents

### Priority Call
Deal **11 damage**.  
Normal Queue advance occurs.

### Reassign the Queue
Deal **9 damage**.  
Apply **1 Panic**.  
Move player backward one position unless Priority prevents it.

### Final Counter Closed
Gain **16 Block**.  
No normal Queue advance.  
Cooldown: **3 boss intents**.

### Crowd Presses Forward
Deal **13 damage**.  
Apply **1 Panic**.

### Last Number of the Day
Deal **18 damage**.  
Cooldown: **3 boss intents**.  
Always telegraphed one full response turn in advance.

## 5.8 Counterplay

The Queue modifies normal combat rather than replacing it.

Offense creates Priority.

Defensive decks may exploit Yield Your Place.

Paperwork-sensitive decks may avoid Petition for Priority.

Service Windows are strong bonuses, not mandatory damage gates.

# 6. The Lord Sealkeeper

**HP:** **136**  
**Role:** selective dismantling of authority / reusable stolen power  
**Difficulty target:** High Act-I boss  
**Mechanical thesis:**

> **Choose which protection disappears first, then decide how to spend the authority you broke loose.**

## 6.1 Character / visual identity

The Lord Sealkeeper is surrounded by three monumental Great Seals.

These are not ordinary wax seals.

They are visible embodiments of state authority.

Each Great Seal should have a distinct visual language and position around the boss.

# Phase I — Certified Authority

The boss begins with:

- **Great Seal of Access**
- **Great Seal of Testimony**
- **Great Seal of Execution**

## 6.2 Seal Ward

At the beginning of each player turn:

> Sealkeeper gains **4 Block per active Great Seal**

Base:

- 3 Seals → 12
- 2 Seals → 8
- 1 Seal → 4

### Great Seal of Access

While active:

> Seal Ward gains an additional **4 Block**.

At combat start with all three Seals:

> total Seal Ward = **16 Block**

### Great Seal of Testimony

The first time each player turn the Sealkeeper receives a negative status:

> gain **5 Block**.

The status applies normally.

No immunity.

Maximum once per player turn.

### Great Seal of Execution

The first direct attack made by the Sealkeeper during each boss turn:

> deals **+4 damage**.

## 6.3 Breaking a Great Seal

The first time each player turn that:

1. the player removes all current Block from the Sealkeeper; and
2. subsequently causes at least 1 actual HP loss,

trigger:

> **BREAK A GREAT SEAL**

The player chooses which active Great Seal breaks.

Maximum:

> **1 Seal Break per player turn**

After the break:

> Sealkeeper becomes **Cracked** until end of player turn.

### Cracked

While Cracked:

> Sealkeeper takes **25% increased player-caused HP damage**.

## 6.4 Broken Seal Fragments

Each deliberately broken Seal creates a matching one-use player-side Fragment.

Fragments:

- cost **0 Energy**;
- are boss-context actions;
- persist through phase transition;
- disappear after use.

### Fragment of Access
Remove up to **12 current Block** from Sealkeeper.

### Fragment of Testimony
Remove up to **2 stacks of one negative status** from the player.

### Fragment of Execution
Reduce the next direct Sealkeeper attack by **8 damage**.

## 6.5 Phase-I intents

### Press the Great Seal
Deal **10 damage**.  
Gain **8 Block**.

### Waxen Authority
Gain **16 Block**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.

### Seal the Record
Deal **8 damage**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.  
Gain **8 Block**.

### Mallet of Validity
Deal **15 damage**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

### Certified Denial
Gain **12 Block**.  
Apply **1 Doubt**.

# Transition — The Keeper Unsealed

Primary trigger:

> all three Great Seals are broken.

Failsafe:

> Sealkeeper reaches **58 HP or less**.

If the HP failsafe triggers while Seals remain:

> all remaining Great Seals shatter without granting Fragments.

Then:

- remove all current Block;
- gain **10 Block**;
- remove remaining Seal effects;
- enter Phase II.

# Phase II — Unsealed Authority

## 6.6 Reclaim the Seal

The Sealkeeper may target one unused Fragment.

The selected Fragment is previewed one full player turn in advance.

If the Fragment still exists when Reclaim resolves:

### Access reclaimed
Gain **16 Block**.

### Testimony reclaimed
Remove up to **2 negative-status stacks** from Sealkeeper.  
Then gain **6 Block**.

### Execution reclaimed
Gain **2 Strength**.

The Fragment is then removed.

If the player spends that Fragment before Reclaim resolves:

> Reclaim fails.

Sealkeeper gains only:

> **5 Block**

A Fragment may be targeted for Reclaim only once.

If no unused Fragment exists:

> Reclaim is not eligible.

## 6.7 Phase-II intents

### Shattered Impression
Deal **13 damage**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.

### Splintered Authority
Deal **10 damage**.  
Gain **10 Block**.

### Unsealed Verdict
Deal **17 damage**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

### Stamp the Flesh
Deal **11 damage**.  
Apply **2 Paperwork**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

## Signature — Authority Without Seal

Unlocked once all three Fragment states are resolved as:

- spent;
- reclaimed;
- or never created because of emergency unsealing.

Deal:

> **21 damage**

Apply:

> **1 Paperwork**

Cooldown:

> **3 boss intents**

Cannot occur twice consecutively.

## 6.8 Counterplay

The player chooses which protection matters most to remove first.

Examples:

- threatened defensive run → break Execution;
- debuff-heavy deck → break Testimony;
- burst deck → break Access.

The Fragments then create a second strategic choice:

> spend now, or risk the Sealkeeper reclaiming them later.

# 7. The Municipal Dragon

**HP:** **142**  
**Role:** permit theft / stored authorization / consequence of incomplete regulation  
**Difficulty target:** High Act-I boss  
**Mechanical thesis:**

> **Steal civic authority from the hoard and use it against an increasingly illegal dragon.**

## 7.1 Character / visual identity

The Municipal Dragon is a classical dragon whose hoard consists of permits, charters, stamped permissions and civic authorizations rather than gold.

The joke should remain immediate and readable.

The dragon must feel physically dangerous, but its real treasure is administrative legitimacy.

# Phase I — The Civic Hoard

Start with:

> **2 Hoarded Permits**

Maximum simultaneous:

> **4**

Maximum additional Permits created during combat:

> **2**

At boss-turn start:

> gain **3 Block per Hoarded Permit**.

## 7.2 Steal Permit

The first time each player turn the Dragon suffers at least:

> **14 player-caused HP loss**

during that turn:

> steal 1 Hoarded Permit, if available.

Maximum:

> **1 Permit stolen per player turn**

A stolen Permit becomes:

> **1 Authorization**

Authorization maximum:

> **3**

If the player already has 3 Authorization:

> the Permit is still removed from the Dragon, but no additional Authorization is gained.

This loss is visibly warned.

## 7.3 Authorization actions

Maximum:

> **1 Authorization action per player turn**

### Authorized Expedition — cost 1
Gain **1 Energy this turn**.

### Authorized Entry — cost 1
Remove up to **12 current Block** from the Dragon.

### Order an Inspection — cost 1
Reveal the Dragon's next intent after its currently previewed intent.

Authorization persists through phase transition.

## 7.4 Phase-I intents

### Civic Claw
Deal **11 damage**.

### Ordinance Breath
Deal **9 damage**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.

### Emergency Permit
If permit-generation charges remain, create **1 Hoarded Permit**.  
Gain **10 Block**.  
Cooldown: **3 boss intents**.  
If creation is unavailable, the intent is ineligible.

### Inspect the Hoard
Gain **1 Strength + 10 Block**.  
Cooldown: **3 boss intents**.

### Dragon's Assessment
Deal **12 + 2 per Hoarded Permit**.  
Maximum: **20 damage**.  
Cooldown: **2 boss intents**.

# Transition — Burn the Registry

Trigger:

> Dragon reaches **78 HP or less**

or:

> player has stolen **4 total Permits**

whichever occurs first.

The next boss action becomes:

## Burn the Registry

Consume all remaining Hoarded Permits.

For each consumed Permit:

> gain **1 Code Violation**

Maximum:

> **4 Code Violations**

Then:

> deal **8 damage**

If at least one Permit was burned:

> apply **1 Paperwork**

All Permit generation ends.

Enter Phase II.

# Phase II — The Unlicensed Dragon

## 7.5 UNLICENSED

Each Code Violation gives:

> **+2 direct attack damage**

Maximum:

> **+8**

## New Authorization action

### Issue a Citation — cost 1
Remove **1 Code Violation**.

All Phase-I Authorization actions remain usable.

## 7.6 Phase-II intents

Values below are base values before UNLICENSED.

### Unlicensed Claw
Deal **12 + UNLICENSED**.

### Fire-Code Violation
Deal **9 + UNLICENSED**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.

### Unsafe Occupancy
Gain **14 Block + 1 Strength**.

### Collapsed Ordinance
Deal **11 + UNLICENSED**.  
Gain **8 Block**.

## Signature — Municipal Inferno

Deal:

> **15 + UNLICENSED**

Maximum:

> **23 damage**

If 3 or more Code Violations remain:

> apply **2 Paperwork**

Otherwise:

> apply **1 Paperwork**

Cooldown:

> **3 boss intents**

Always telegraphed one full player turn before resolution.

## 7.7 Counterplay

Phase-I performance determines Phase-II danger.

Stealing Permits:

- reduces Hoard Block;
- weakens Dragon's Assessment;
- reduces future Code Violations;
- grants reusable Authorization.

The player may save Authorization for Phase II or spend it immediately for tempo.

# 8. The Living Charter

**HP:** **134**  
**Role:** rule rewriting / judicial review / constitutional interpretation  
**Difficulty target:** Highest mechanical complexity in Act I  
**Mechanical thesis:**

> **Combat rules are law. Law can be reviewed, amended and interpreted.**

## 8.1 Character / visual identity

The Living Charter is an animate legal document whose clauses physically rewrite themselves during combat.

Visual direction may include:

- self-writing script;
- seals functioning as joints;
- paragraphs folding into limbs;
- Articles physically entering the combat UI;
- words crossing themselves out during Judicial Review;
- margins expanding during Emergency Amendment.

The Charter is not a clerk carrying law.

It is law made hostile and alive.

## 8.2 Constitutional Article selection

The complete boss has:

> **6 possible Articles**

At combat start:

> select **3 Articles** for that encounter.

All three selected Articles and their full rules are visible from combat start.

They may be displayed as:

- Published;
- Prepared;
- Prepared.

The player is never expected to memorize an unseen Article.

## 8.3 Article pool

Each Article must remain explainable in one short rule statement.

### Article of Continuance
At turn end, both sides retain **50% of remaining Block**, rounded down.

Retained Block is not considered newly gained Block unless a specific Conflict interpretation says otherwise.

### Article of Due Notice
Newly applied negative statuses become active at the start of the recipient's next turn instead of immediately.

Pending statuses remain visible. Pending statuses may be cleansed before activation.

Activation does not count as a second status application.

### Article of Redress
The first time each side loses at least **14 HP during one turn**, that side gains **8 Remedy Block** at the beginning of its next turn.

Maximum once per side per round.

### Article of Mutual Security
The first time either side gains Block during a turn, the opposing side gains **4 Block**.

Maximum once for each side per turn.

The 4 Block generated by Mutual Security does not trigger Mutual Security again.

### Article of Reciprocal Burden
The first time each turn a combatant successfully applies a negative status to the opponent, the applier receives **1 Doubt**.

Maximum once per side per turn.

This Article affects the Charter as well as the player.

### Article of Full Disclosure
While active:

- the player sees the top **2 cards** of the Draw Pile;
- the player sees the Charter's **current intent and following intent**.

No hidden downside is attached.

This Article is intentionally capable of favouring the player.

The Charter represents law, not a collection of one-sided curses.

# Phase I — Published Law

At combat start:

> publish the first prepared Article.

Only **1 Active Article** may exist during Phase I.

## 8.4 Phase-I intents

### Clause Writes Itself
Deal **8 damage**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.

### Ambiguous Provision
Apply **1 Doubt**.  
Gain **12 Block**.

### Strict Construction
Deal **14 damage**.

### Constitutional Reserve
Gain **16 Block**.

### Read Against You

## 8.5 Judicial Review

After **2 ordinary Charter actions** under the same Active Article:

> the next Charter intent becomes **Judicial Review**.

### Judicial Review
Deal **6 damage**.

Before the player's next normal turn, offer:

#### UPHOLD
The current Article remains active.  
Player gains **6 Block**.

#### STRIKE DOWN
The current Article becomes inactive.  
The next prepared Article becomes active.  
Charter gains **8 Block**.

No additional Amendment Authority resource is used.

Strike Down therefore exchanges one known law for another rather than simply deleting the mechanic.

# Transition — Emergency Amendment

Trigger:

> after **2 completed Judicial Reviews**

or failsafe:

> Charter reaches **67 HP or less**

The next boss action becomes:

## Emergency Amendment
Deal **8 damage**.  
Apply **1 Paperwork**.  
Gain **8 Block**.

Then Phase II begins.

# Phase II — Contradictory Constitution

Maximum:

> **2 simultaneous Active Articles**

Never three ordinary Articles.

If the transition occurred through the second Judicial Review:

> retain the current Article and publish the next prepared Article.

If the HP failsafe occurs earlier:

> publish the next prepared Article beside the current one.

## 8.6 Phase-II Judicial Review

After **3 ordinary Charter actions**, the Charter nominates one Active Article for Judicial Review.

The nominated Article is visible.

UPHOLD and STRIKE DOWN function as above.

If STRIKE DOWN is chosen:

> replace that nominated Article with the currently inactive prepared Article.

Across the entire fight, the player only ever needs to understand the same three selected Article texts.

## 8.7 Conflict of Law

Only predefined Article pairs create special Conflicts.

No hidden emergent interaction is allowed.

When a Conflict pair first becomes simultaneously active:

> the player selects one interpretation.

That interpretation remains fixed for that pair for the rest of the combat.

The player is not asked again each trigger.

### Conflict: Continuance + Mutual Security

Choose:

**Retention Is Not Acquisition**  
Retained Block does **not** trigger Mutual Security.

or:

**Retention Is Renewed Security**  
Retained Block counts as a new Block gain at turn start for Mutual Security.

### Conflict: Due Notice + Reciprocal Burden

Choose:

**Burden on Filing**  
Reciprocal Burden applies when the pending status is originally applied.

or:

**Burden on Effect**  
Reciprocal Burden applies when the pending status activates.

Only one interpretation is used.

Deal **9 damage**.

Deal **16 damage**.

### Living Commentary
Deal **12 damage**.

# 9. Final Pool Audit

## 9.1 Mechanical separation

The five bosses occupy distinct spaces.

### Deputy Undersecretary — Workload management
Player choices determine which unresolved obligations become Phase-II problems.

### Queue Commissioner — Position management
The player manipulates access to repeated vulnerability windows.

### Lord Sealkeeper — Selective authority dismantling
The player chooses which protection disappears and later manages stolen Fragments.

### Municipal Dragon — Resource theft
The player steals the boss's own civic authority and carries it across phases.

### Living Charter — Rule rewriting
The player learns, reviews and interprets temporary modifications to fundamental combat rules.

No two bosses ask the same primary player question.

## 9.2 Archetype audit

### Burst / Big-hit decks
Strong interaction with Great Seal breaking, Permit theft, Priority generation and Performance Matters.

Not required for victory against Charter or Deputy.

### Multi-hit decks
No mechanic grants repeated threshold progress from multiple hits in the same player turn.

Permit theft, Priority and Seal Break are capped appropriately.

### Defensive decks
Can resolve defensive Matters, use Queue positioning, break Execution Seal first, preserve Authorization for Phase II, and exploit or adapt to Charter Articles.

No boss requires constant burst damage to remain playable.

### Status / debuff decks
Lord Sealkeeper initially moderates status pressure but allows the player to break Testimony first.

The Charter's status Articles are symmetric and visible.

No boss possesses blanket debuff immunity.

### Scaling decks
All five bosses possess anti-stall escalation, but none use an arbitrary automatic death timer.

Examples:

- Backlog;
- repeated Queue cycles;
- Unsealed Authority;
- Permit generation / Code Violations;
- Constitutional Crisis.

## 9.3 Information-load audit

Recommended persistent UI load:

| Boss | Main visible systems |
|---|---|
| Deputy | Desk / Matters + Backlog or Executive Files |
| Commissioner | Queue Track + Priority + Service state |
| Sealkeeper | Seals or Fragments |
| Dragon | Permits + Authorization + Code Violations |
| Charter | Active Articles + interpretation / Exception |

The Charter is intentionally the most information-dense Act-I boss.

Its complexity is bounded through:

- only three selected Articles per combat;
- only one Active Article in Phase I;
- at most two Active Articles in Phase II;
- only predefined Conflict pairs;
- no third Article in the final state.

## 9.4 Softlock / exploit audit

Required safeguards:

### Deputy
- maximum three active Matters;
- Backlog capped at five;
- individual Matters must be deterministically possible;
- no fourth Matter can be forced into existence.

### Commissioner
- boss takes normal damage outside Service;
- Counter Closed has a cooldown;
- Priority prevents only boss-forced backward movement;
- queue movement is always visible.

### Sealkeeper
- maximum one Seal Break per player turn;
- phase transition has HP failsafe;
- unused Fragment reclaim is telegraphed;
- Fragments cost no Energy.

### Dragon
- maximum one Permit stolen per player turn;
- maximum two additional Permit generations;
- Authorization capped at three;
- Code Violations capped at four.

### Charter
- all selected Articles visible from combat start;
- Mutual Security is non-recursive;
- status activation does not duplicate application triggers;
- Conflict interpretation is chosen once per pair;
- Exceptions capped at one per side.

## 9.5 Encounter-duration audit

Boss HP is intentionally moderate.

Once the player has solved the boss system, combat should be allowed to end.

No boss receives:

- permanent percentage damage reduction;
- repeated resurrection;
- arbitrary invulnerability;
- infinitely regenerating protection;
- or mandatory multi-minute post-solution phases.

Phase II should deepen the Phase-I system, not restart the fight with a second unrelated health sponge.

If none exist:

This prevents invisible deterministic sniping of one irreplaceable card while preserving forced custody.

# 4. Shared Act-III Boss Rules

## 4.1 Universal vocabulary

Boss-local systems do not become new Act-wide mechanics.

Universal vocabulary remains:

- Safe-Conduct
- Trespass
- Claim
- Wergild

Local systems such as Ground, Counterseal, Remembered Favor, Buried Claim and Favor are encounter-specific.

## 4.2 Claim semantics

A Claim may carry local metadata such as Road/Root Ground.

That metadata does not make it a different universal status.

Claim transfer:

> is never Claim creation.

Ground change:

> is neither transfer nor creation.

## 4.3 Wergild

Normal `Make Amends` rules remain available unless a visible boss rule explicitly modifies them.

A boss may never create a state in which all legal Wergild payment routes are simultaneously unavailable.

## 4.4 Boss-specific transitions

There is no generic shared 50%-HP phase rule.

Primary transitions arise from:

- complaints being heard;
- precedent becoming established;
- hospitality being accepted;
- terrain thresholds being crossed;
- or courtly reciprocity progressing.

HP thresholds are used only as failsafes or terrain-specific mechanics.

## 4.5 Transition timing

Finish the current card/damage resolution first.

Then queue the transition as the next legal boss-state action.

No mid-card phase changes.

## 4.6 Telegraphing

Major Claim cash-outs, threshold consequences, House Rules, Royal Orders and signatures are shown at least one complete player response window before resolution.

## 4.7 Source cleanup

Boss death removes:

- source-bound Trespass;
- open boss Wergild;
- local counters;
- temporary permissions;
- pending boss-only laws.

No local legal state leaks into the next combat.

exist only in their encounter.

## 5.2 Solvability filter

A boss-generated behavioral requirement must never be knowingly impossible from the current deterministic state.

This applies especially to:

- Royal Commands
- Blueprints
- Rations
- any exact Energy requirement
- any card-type sequencing requirement

If the intended rule is not currently achievable:

> substitute a valid alternate from that boss's defined pool.

No boss requires an unknown future draw.

---

## 5.3 Player agency before punishment

A major boss resource should generally provide:

- a prevention route;
- a mitigation route;
- a conversion route;
- or a deliberate risk/reward choice.

The intended design is not:

> obey or suffer.

It is:

> choose how much of the institution to engage with.

---

## 5.4 Transition timing

A transition never interrupts:

- a card;
- a multi-hit batch;
- a Weighed resolution;
- an Inscribed amplification;
- or a status-removal event.

Finish the current resolution.

Then queue the boss-state transition as the next legal encounter action.

---

## 5.5 Telegraphing

Major signatures and state cash-outs are always visible before the player turn that can meaningfully answer them.

Examples:

- exposed Royal Name
- Heart Declared Light
- Capstone Descends
- Stores Stand Open
- Vessel Unseal
- King's Mouth signature
- Black Flood

---

## 5.6 Non-recursion

Boss-local direct HP Loss or secondary effects do not recursively count as:

- a new card hit;
- a new status removal;
- a new Inscribed application;
- or a new boss-resource trigger.

---

# Act V – The Divine Ledger

## Status

**Content Redesign: Complete**

Act V is the final regular act of *Bureaucrats and Broomsticks*.

It is not a normal act and deliberately abandons the standard structure used in Acts I–IV.

Act V is a final divine gauntlet in which the player enters with the build created over the entire run and receives no meaningful opportunity to repair fundamental weaknesses.

---

# 1. Core Act Structure

## Act Name

**Act V – The Divine Ledger**

## Structure

**Boss → Boss → Boss**

At the beginning of Act V:

- 3 different bosses are selected from a pool of 6.
- No boss may repeat within the same run.
- Boss order is variable.
- The selected bosses and their order are visible from the beginning of the act.

There are:

- no standard enemies,
- no elites,
- no normal events,
- no shop,
- no treasure,
- no healing between fights,
- no card rewards between bosses,
- no relic rewards between bosses,
- no gold rewards between bosses.

The build that enters Act V is the build that must defeat Act V.

Boss-internal resources and temporary combat systems reset after each boss.

Persistent HP loss remains.

---

# 2. Design Philosophy

Act V continues the anti-chronological development of bureaucracy.

By this point, bureaucracy is no longer a human institution.

The cosmos itself is administration.

Things happen because they were:

- named,
- counted,
- written,
- decreed,
- measured,
- witnessed,
- assigned.

The player no longer encounters officials.

The player encounters the gods from whose cosmic order administration itself emerged.

The act is:

- Mesopotamian,
- Sumerian / Akkadian inspired,
- divine,
- archaic,
- alien,
- monumental,
- cosmic,
- relentless.

The gods are inspired by historical mythology, but translated freely into the absurd magical-bureaucratic world of *Bureaucrats and Broomsticks*.

Act V is intentionally extremely difficult.

A very strong run may reach Act V and still fail.

That is acceptable and intended.

---

# 3. Global Boss Design Rules

Each boss should feel almost like a separate game mode.

Bosses may use:

- unique resources,
- unique UI areas,
- multi-turn planning,
- phase-specific rules,
- temporary overrides of normal combat logic,
- special counters,
- altered card sequencing,
- stored future actions,
- stored past actions,
- combat-wide rule changes.

However:

- all unusual rules must be clearly telegraphed,
- hidden gotcha mechanics should be avoided,
- extreme difficulty is allowed,
- incomprehensibility is not.

A player should initially think:

> “What the hell are the rules here?”

But once the rules are understood, the fight must become:

- readable,
- strategic,
- learnable,
- masterable.

---

# 4. Shared UI Principle

Every Act-V boss receives a prominent **Divine Rule Area** in the combat UI.

This area always occupies a consistent location, but its content changes completely by boss.

Examples:

- Nisaba → **The First Tablet**
- Inanna → **Eanna Ledger**
- Nanshe → **The Ration Tablet**
- Nanna-Sin → **The Lunar Calendar**
- Utu → **Oaths / Witness**
- Enlil → **Decrees**

The player should immediately understand:

> This is where the god explains what reality currently means.

# 6. Nisaba – Keeper of the First Tablet

## Core Fantasy

Nisaba is the divine origin of written order.

She does not merely record reality.

**What is written becomes real.**

Her fight asks:

> Can you prevent what has already been written from becoming true?

## Personality

- calm,
- exact,
- patient,
- almost gentle,
- never theatrical.

She is not angry when the player resists.

She is mildly interested when the player proves capable of correction.

She increasingly speaks as if the player’s future were already a completed record.

---

## Visual Identity

Primary motifs:

- clay,
- reed stylus,
- grain,
- cuneiform,
- tablets,
- lapis lazuli,

### Phase II

Darkness and luminous cuneiform.

# 6.1 Unique Rule – The First Tablet

A large tablet is visible beside Nisaba.

It contains several future statements.

Example:

| The First Tablet | Enactment |
|---|---:|
| `Three Measures Shall Be Withheld.` | 1 turn |
| `The Body Shall Bear Thirty-Six.` | 2 turns |
| `Three Wounds Shall Be Entered.` | 3 turns |

These are **Inscriptions**.

They are not standard intents or statuses.

When their countdown expires, the written statement becomes true.

---

# 6.2 Reed Marks

The player receives a boss-specific resource:

## `Reed Marks`

Suggested maximum:

**4**

Baseline gain:

**+1 per player turn**

A Reed Mark can be spent to revise an active inscription.

The player does not dispel the effect.

The player edits the text.

Example:

### `The Body Shall Bear Thirty-Six`

| Revisions | Result |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Lose 36 HP |
| 1 | Lose 24 HP |
| 2 | Lose 12 HP |
| 3 | No effect |
| 4 | Nisaba loses 18 HP |

The final revision may reverse subject or meaning.

The written statement still becomes true.

Only the wording changed.

---

# 6.3 Counted Margin

Each player turn has a visible required card count.

Example:

> **Margin: IV**

If the player ends the turn after exactly 4 played cards:

**+1 additional Reed Mark next turn**

Important:

- this is always a bonus,
- never a punishment,
- failure only means losing the bonus.

The player may play more or fewer cards freely.

---

# 6.4 Inscriptions

Recommended base inscription pool:

### `The Body Shall Bear Thirty-Six`

HP loss.

Possible revisions:

36 → 24 → 12 → 0 → damage Nisaba.

---

### `Three Measures Shall Be Withheld`

Next affected turn:

-3 Energy → -2 → -1 → 0 → +1 Energy.

---

### `The Hand Shall Hold Two`

Next affected draw is restricted.

Possible progression:

2 → 3 → 4 → normal → +1 draw.

---

### `The Guard Shall Be Counted as Nothing`

Future Block is reduced or erased.

Possible progression:

100% lost → 75% → 50% → no effect → gain Block.

---

### `Three Wounds Shall Be Entered`

---

### `Two Works Shall Be Broken`

  > “The RNG generated an impossible board.”

# 6.8 Phase II – The Lapis Record

Approx. **65–30% HP**

Transition concept:

> **“Wet earth accepts correction.”**

> **“Then let us write upon something less forgiving.”**

The clay tablet is replaced by a lapis tablet.

New mechanic:

## `Impressed Seal`

Nisaba can telegraph that she will seal one inscription.

A sealed inscription cannot be revised for one full player turn.

The target is always shown in advance.

The player must decide which inscription must be revised before it becomes temporarily untouchable.

# 6.9 Phase III – The Last Line

At approx. **30% HP**

All ordinary inscriptions are closed.

This is intentional.

The finale should focus on exactly one sentence.

The tablet becomes:

# `THE LAST LINE`

> **`On the Fourth Dawn, the Name of the Supplicant Shall Be Erased.`**

Countdown:

**4 rounds**

Possible revisions:

| Revision | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Player is erased — lethal |
| 1 | Massive HP loss |
| 2 | Heavy HP loss |
| 3 | Moderate HP loss |
| 4 | `The Name of the Supplicant Shall Remain.` |
| 5 | `The Name of the Keeper Shall Be Erased.` |

During this phase Nisaba gains:

## `Indelible`

While the Last Line is at Revision **0–3**:

> Nisaba's HP cannot fall below 1.

The protection is tied to the unresolved lethal wording, not to the countdown itself.

### Revision 4 — `The Name of the Supplicant Shall Remain.`

As soon as Revision 4 is reached:

> remove `Indelible` immediately.

The Last Line may remain visible until its countdown resolves, but Nisaba can now be killed normally. A player who has already solved the sentence is never forced to endure several additional gauntlet rounds solely because the timer has not expired.

### Revision 5 — `The Name of the Keeper Shall Be Erased.`

As soon as Revision 5 is reached:

> resolve the rewritten sentence against Nisaba immediately.

Nisaba loses all remaining HP and the combat ends after the current action finishes resolving.

The player can therefore:

- survive by revising to `Shall Remain` and finish the fight normally;
- or perfectly reverse the sentence for an immediate legal defeat of the Keeper.

# 6.10 Key Lines

Intro:

> **“Do not speak.”**

> **“A spoken thing may still change.”**

# 8. Nanshe – Keeper of the Just Ration

## Core Fantasy

Nanshe represents justice, correct measure, fair distribution and exact social allocation.

She is not cruel.

She is perfectly fair.

That is what makes her terrifying.

Her fight asks:

> Can your build survive when every resource you consume today is taken from the exact amount available tomorrow?

---

## Personality

Nanshe is:

- calm,
- compassionate,
- firm,
- transparent,
- never vindictive.

She may genuinely help the player when the measure requires it.

She simply never grants unfair exceptions.

---

## Visual Identity

Primary motifs:

- water,
- reed,
- marsh,
- grain,
- baskets,
- weights,
- fish,
- birds,
- low storehouses,
- balanced scales.

A massive scale behind her remains almost perfectly level throughout the fight.

---

# 8.1 Unique Rule – The Just Ration

Combat is organized into:

# `Distributions`

Each Distribution covers:

**3 player turns**

At the start of each Distribution, Nanshe reveals the entire:

# `RATION TABLET`

Example:

| | Day I | Day II | Day III |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Energy | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Draw | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Nanshe | 18 dmg | 12×2 dmg | 34 dmg |

The player sees:

- all three resource allotments,
- all three upcoming Nanshe actions.

Nanshe does not hide her future intents within a Distribution.

---

# 8.2 Build-Relative Fairness

The ration is based on the player’s actual baseline.

Example:

If the build naturally receives:

- 4 Energy,
- 6 Draw,

then over three days the base ration is approximately:

- 12 Energy,
- 18 Draw.

Nanshe does not flatten every build to the same baseline.

She recognizes what the player has legitimately acquired.

---

# 8.3 Take Ahead

The player may borrow from a later day.

Example:

| | I | II | III |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Energy | 4 | 4 | 4 |

becomes:

| | I | II | III |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Energy | 6 | 2 | 4 |

Total amount remains unchanged.

No additional punishment occurs.

Tomorrow is simply smaller.

---

# 8.4 Draw Allocation

Draw can also be moved between days.

The player may take more now and reduce future draw.

The player may also voluntarily take less now and preserve draw for later.

The system reallocates **draw quantity**, not specific future cards.

---

# 8.5 Return the Portion

Unused natural resources may be moved forward.

Example:

If the player receives 4 Energy but uses only 3:

the unused Energy may move to the next day.

Nanshe does not confiscate unused allotments.

---

# 8.6 Borrowed Measure

If the player consumes more than remains in the entire current Distribution:

the deficit becomes:

## `Borrowed Measure`

Example:

`Borrowed Measure: Energy II`

The next Distribution is reduced accordingly.

Debt may survive across multiple Distributions.

---

# 8.7 Minimum Daily Ration

Audit rule:

Outside the Final Distribution, a day should retain at least:

- **1 natural Energy**
- **1 natural Draw**

If debt would reduce a day below this, the unpaid deficit continues into later days or future Distributions.

This preserves minimal player agency.

No debt is forgiven.

---

# 8.8 Phase I – The Measured Portion

Approx. **100–65% HP**

The ration controls:

- natural turn-start Energy,
- natural turn-start Draw.

Additional Energy or Draw generated by cards and relics is still free during Phase I.

The player learns the distribution system.

---

# 8.9 Distribution Patterns

Nanshe does not use a simple repetitive cycle.

Instead, each Distribution reveals a full pattern.

Examples:

### `Distribution of Shelter`

- Day I – heavy attack
- Day II – defensive action
- Day III – heavy attack

### `Distribution of Labor`

- multi-hit
- medium attack
- large attack

### `Distribution of Rest`

- very large attack
- defensive day
- defensive day

### `Distribution of Need`

- medium attack
- medium attack
- extreme final attack

The full pattern is visible before Day I.

---

# 8.10 Phase II – Count Every Measure

Approx. **65–30% HP**

Transition concept:

> **“Three measures entered.”**

> **“They were not allotted.”**

> **“That does not make them uncounted.”**

Now:

- additional Energy generation is counted,
- additional Draw is counted.

Example:

`Draw 3`

pulls 3 Draw from later rations.

`Gain 2 Energy`

pulls 2 Energy from later rations.

Important:

Nanshe counts **resource quantity**, not efficiency.

Therefore:

- Cost Reduction remains legitimate,
- Retain remains legitimate,
- already-present card copies are not Draw,
- clever sequencing is not reclassified as hidden ration.

---

# 8.11 Seal the Basket

Nanshe can telegraph:

## `Seal the Basket`

One future daily ration becomes fixed.

The player cannot manually move resources into or out of that day.

Generated resources during that day may still draw from later portions.

The target day is always announced in advance.

---

# 8.12 The Measure Holds

If a full Distribution is completed cleanly:

- without new Borrowed Measure,
- without overconsumption,
- without structural imbalance,

the player may receive a small stabilization reward.

Example:

- remove 1 existing Borrowed Measure,
- or gain a small amount of Block next Distribution.

This should remain modest.

---

# 8.13 Phase III – The Final Distribution

Approx. **30% HP**

Transition:

> **“There will be four more.”**

> **“There is no fifth.”**

All remaining combat resources are allocated into one final store.

Example:

# FINAL RATION

**Energy: 16**

**Draw: 20**

# FOUR DAYS REMAIN

The player decides how much of the remaining Energy and Draw to consume each day.

All generated Draw and Energy now also consume from the final store.

---

# 8.14 The Empty Bowl

If the final store reaches:

- Energy 0,
- Draw 0,

then no additional natural supply exists.

Unlike earlier phases, the minimum-ration protection is disabled.

After the four allotted days:

there is no fifth portion.

A player may still act with:

- retained cards,
- already-present cards,
- 0-cost effects,
- other legal resources.

This acts as a soft enrage.

# `— I II III IV III II I`

| Phase | Count |
|---|---:|
| New Moon | — |
| Waxing Crescent | I |
| First Quarter | II |
| Waxing Gibbous | III |
| Full Moon | IV |
| Waning Gibbous | III |
| Last Quarter | II |
| Waning Crescent | I |

The Count determines which played card becomes:

## `Counted`

Example:

At `First Quarter – II`:

the second card played is Counted.

---

# 9.3 The Count Returns

Audit revision:

A Counted card does **not** necessarily wait for the same named lunar phase next orbit.

It returns at the next occurrence of the same **Lunar Count**.

Therefore:

| Count | Recorded | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| I | Waxing Crescent | Waning Crescent |
| II | First Quarter | Last Quarter |
| III | Waxing Gibbous | Waning Gibbous |
| IV | Full Moon | next Full Moon |

This creates different time horizons.

short-term preparation.

### II
medium-term.

### I
long-term.

### IV
full-orbit investment.

a temporary copy of the Counted card enters the player hand.

The copy:

- retains upgrade state,
- disappears at turn end,
- may be played normally.

It is not automatically cast.

The player remains in control.

When that Count returns:

his old move returns as a weaker:

## `Returning Move`

Suggested strength:

The returning move resolves in addition to his current intent.

---

# 9.6 Full Moon – Perfect Reflection

Full Moon is the combat apex.

Suggested rule:

# `Perfect Reflection`

Lunar Echoes played by the player gain a moderate numerical enhancement.

Nanna-Sin’s Returning Moves are similarly enhanced.

The Full Moon therefore amplifies both sides of recurrence.

the fifth card is genuinely unavailable.

The rule is not a promise.

It is current reality.

---

# 11.2 Unalterable

Decrees cannot be:

- removed,
- dispelled,
- rewritten,
- shortened,
- delayed,
- bargained away.

They expire only according to their announced duration.

---

# 11.3 Next Decree

Every normal Decree is announced at least one full player turn before enactment.

Example:

# `NEXT DECREE`

> `NO WORK SHALL RETURN.`

> Played cards enter Exhaust instead of Discard.

> Enacts next round.

The player may prepare.

Enlil is absolute, not arbitrary.

---

# 11.4 Universal Word

Where wording is universal, Decrees apply to both sides.

Example:

### `NO SINGLE BLOW SHALL EXCEED TWENTY`

Any single damage instance is capped at 20.

This affects:

- player,
- Enlil.

Enlil never cancels his own Decree because it disadvantages him.

# 11.5 Decree Families

## Order of Works

### `THE FOURTH WORK SHALL BE THE LAST`

Maximum 4 cards played each turn.

---

### `NO WORK SHALL FOLLOW ITS LIKENESS`

The same card type may not be played twice consecutively.

---

### `THE FIRST WORD SHALL BE WITHOUT PRICE`

First card each turn costs 0.

---

### `THE THIRD WORK SHALL BE WITHOUT PRICE`

Third card each turn costs 0.

---

## Order of Measure

### `NO WORK SHALL BE WITHOUT MEASURE`

Cards cannot cost 0.

Any card that would cost 0 instead costs 1.

---

### `WHAT IS UNSPENT SHALL PASS INTO TOMORROW`

Unused Energy is retained between turns.

---

### `THE MEASURE SHALL NOT EXCEED SEVEN`

Energy cannot exceed 7.

Optional pool candidate.

---

## Order of Returning

### `NO WORK SHALL RETURN`

Played cards enter Exhaust instead of Discard.

Audit rule:

Outside the final phase this should normally last only **1 round**, because its consequences persist after expiration.

---

### `WHAT HAS BEEN CAST OUT SHALL RETURN`

At turn start, one Exhausted card returns to hand.

---

### `WHAT IS DISCARDED SHALL NOT BE RECALLED`

Cards discarded by player effects enter Exhaust.

This should be treated as a severe Decree.

---

## Order of Force

### `NO SINGLE BLOW SHALL EXCEED TWENTY`

Single damage instances are capped at 20.

Universal.

---

### `NO WALL SHALL RISE ABOVE THIRTY`

Block cannot exceed 30.

Universal.

---

### `THE FIRST BLOW SHALL FALL UPON NOTHING`

The first damage instance dealt by each side each round becomes 0.

Universal.

---

## Order of the Hand

### `SEVEN SHALL BE THE HAND'S MEASURE`

Hand size cannot exceed 7.

Cards beyond this are not drawn, rather than discarded.

---

### `THE LAST DRAWN SHALL REMAIN`

The final naturally drawn card each turn gains Retain.

Potential positive / ambivalent Decree.

---

# 11.6 Decree Director

Enlil should use around 12 carefully designed Decrees.

The Director must prevent:

- contradictory Decrees,
- mathematically impossible states,
- combinations that completely hard-lock a legitimate build.

However:

- harsh matchups remain allowed,
- severe anti-synergy remains allowed,
- the Director is not meant to make Enlil nice.

---

# 11.7 Decree Severity

Audit rule:

Decrees should have internal severity classes.

## Structural / Minor

Typically 2–3 rounds.

Examples:

- first card costs 0,
- Energy carries,
- sequencing rules.

## Severe

Typically 1 round outside the final phase.

Examples:

- played cards Exhaust,
- zero-cost prevention,
- powerful zone rewrites.

This limits permanent downstream damage from supposedly temporary rules.

# 14. Boss-Order Philosophy

Act V uses three random bosses from the six-boss pool.

The selected order is visible before the first fight.

This matters because:

- there is no healing,
- HP loss persists,
- the player may strategically accept damage in one boss to preserve strength for another.

Example:

A player entering:

**Utu → Nanna-Sin → Enlil**

can plan differently than one entering:

**Inanna → Nanshe → Nisaba**

Boss knowledge therefore becomes part of Act-V strategy.

# 15. Potentially Harsh Gauntlets

These are allowed.

The goal is not equal difficulty for every build.

- commitment under pressure,
- full adaptation.

Very punishing to imprecise play.

---

## Inanna → Nanna-Sin → Enlil

Tests:

- engine greed,
- long-term sequencing,
- rule reconstruction.

Dangerous for complex combo builds.

---

## Nisaba → Nanshe → Utu

Can create heavy HP attrition.

The player may:

- accept Inscriptions,
- ration defensive resources,
- later decide whether breaking an Oath is worth surviving.

---

# 16. Matchup Philosophy

Not every boss should be equally hard for every build.

Examples:

- Draw-heavy engines may hate Nanshe.
- 0-cost spam may hate Enlil.
- Compact efficient decks may perform well against Utu.
- High-value engines may exploit Inanna but accumulate enormous Due.
- Nanna-Sin rewards decks capable of intentional sequencing.
- Nisaba remains broadly dangerous because nearly every build must prioritize future threats.

Allowed:

- very hard matchup,
- terrible matchup,
- awkward matchup.

Not allowed:

- mathematically unwinnable matchup for an otherwise legitimate build.

# 19. Final Canonical Audit Adjustments

The following audit decisions are part of the final design.

## Nisaba

At `The Last Line`:

all ordinary Inscriptions are closed.

The finale focuses entirely on the final sentence.

---

## Inanna

Claim discount applies only to the first play of that card instance each turn.

This avoids unintended free infinite loops.

Offerings are simplified so Energy is mainly offered at turn end and card Dedication occurs around Processions.

---

## Nanshe

Outside the Final Distribution:

minimum natural ration is:

- 1 Energy,
- 1 Draw.

Debt is delayed rather than forgiven.

The protection disappears in the Final Distribution.

---

## Nanna-Sin

Echoes return at the next matching **Lunar Count**, not only the next identical named phase.

This makes the mechanic relevant much earlier and turns the Count itself into a true timing system.

---

## Utu

Witness affects multiple offensive moves.

`Call the Witness` occurs on a predictable rhythm.

Breaking Oaths remains viable, but repeated breaches progressively change the threat landscape.

---

## Enlil

Decrees are internally classified by severity.

Rules with lasting structural consequences are shorter outside Phase III.

The Decree Director prevents hard-lock combinations but does not prevent brutal matchups.
