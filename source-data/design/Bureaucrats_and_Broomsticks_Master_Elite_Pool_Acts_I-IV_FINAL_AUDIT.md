## 3. Final Naming Rule

Elite names should feel like **encounter identities or institutional/mythic titles**, not like prose summaries of the mechanic. The final elite roster no longer uses `The X That/Who...` as an identity pattern. Former sentence-names have been replaced by identities such as `Reopening-Hours Monolith`, `Appellate Staircase`, `Living Petition Chorus`, `Black-Ink Oracle`, `Presentless Clock`, `Surveyor of the Errant Cord` and `The Tombbreakers Three`.

The naming rule is not a ban on long titles. It is a ban on **repetitive descriptive syntax** when a stronger noun identity exists.

---

## 4. Cross-Act Elite Philosophy

Across Acts I–IV, an elite encounter must do more than scale a standard enemy upward.

An elite should:

- create one memorable combat identity;
- reinterpret the act's existing vocabulary rather than simply stack more statuses;
- preserve normal deck actions as the primary solution space;
- expose its important rules visibly;
- avoid archetype hard-locks;
- bind delayed consequences to their source;
- define cleanup and death ordering explicitly;
- cap local resources and escalation loops;
- create a player decision that remains recognizable after the fight ends.

The intended escalation is:

> **Standard enemies teach a rule.**  
> **Elites force the player to negotiate with that rule.**  
> **Bosses create a rule world around it.**

## 1. Final Audit Verdict

Act I uses a final pool of **10 elite encounters**.

The pool was audited after the complete redesign of the Act-I standard encounter pool. The final standard-combat reference contains **25 standard-enemy identities / 32 standard encounter templates**, with solo HP reaching 69 and the hardest late standard duo reaching 88 combined HP.

The elite pool therefore does **not** use large HP inflation as its primary difficulty lever.

Instead:

> **An elite introduces a memorable combat rule.**

The ten final encounters test ten meaningfully different player questions:

| # | Elite | Primary player question |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | The Three Appointments | Which future deadline can I afford to leave alive? |
| 2 | Reopening-Hours Monolith | How much damage do I invest now when it will only be processed later? |
| 3 | Devouring Waiting Room | How much unused Energy may become future danger? |
| 4 | Appellate Staircase | Do I shorten the court structure even though doing so accelerates Final Ruling? |
| 5 | Living Petition Chorus | Which immediate benefits are worth signing into future liabilities? |
| 6 | The Remanded Case | Do I accept a remand or force an aggressive final disposition? |
| 7 | Iron Warrant Avatar | Do I obey an inefficient order or deliberately accept Contempt? |
| 8 | The Seizure Procession | Which part of the confiscation machine do I disrupt first? |
| 9 | Portcullis Judicator | How aggressively do I open a gate that exposes both sides? |
| 10 | Final Notice Knight | Do I slow the deadline, acknowledge service, or accept full enforcement? |

No final elite is merely a standard enemy with increased HP or increased status values.

## 2. Pool-Level Balance Frame

### Standard-combat reference

Act-I standard enemies establish roughly the following ceiling:

- solo enemies: approximately **25–69 HP**;
- common direct attacks: approximately **5–12 damage**;
- large telegraphed standard attacks: approximately **12–18 damage**;
- common Block values: approximately **6–18**;
- hardest finalized standard duo: **88 combined HP**.

### Elite target frame

The final elite encounters sit mostly around:

- **86–98 base combined HP**;
- ordinary individual attacks: approximately **5–13 damage**;
- major telegraphed signatures: approximately **15–20 damage** before contextual modifiers;
- difficulty primarily from unique rules, action economy, timing, target priority and player choice.

The approximate base combined-HP average of the final pool is **~92 HP**.

This is intentional.

> Difficulty comes from the rule first and the numbers second.

# 4. Shared Elite Safety and Resolution Rules

These rules are binding unless an encounter explicitly overrides them.

## 4.1 Telegraphing

Every major delayed consequence must be visible at least one player action window before resolution.

This includes:

- Appointment Due penalties;
- Time Eats Back;
- Final Ruling;
- Read Into the Record;
- Enforce the Deadline;
- Office reopening;
- Gate Height state changes.

No signature may appear as an unpreviewed surprise because of a hidden trigger.

---

## 4.2 Source binding and death cleanup

Encounter-local resources are source-bound unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Examples:

- Appointment Due disappears when its Appointment dies.
- Lost Time belongs to the Devouring Waiting Room.
- Inventoried / Seized belong to the Lantern / Cart process.
- Final Notice belongs to the Final Notice Knight.
- Compliance Order and Contempt belong to the Iron Warrant Avatar.

Death cleanup occurs before a dead source may create new future obligations.

---

## 4.3 Damage-event recursion

Effects such as:

- Pending Business cash-out;
- Compliance Credit overflow;
- other explicitly defined direct HP Loss;

are **HP Loss, not new Damage events**.

They do not recursively trigger DamageDealt / DamageReceived effects unless explicitly stated.

---

## 4.4 Damage-threshold state changes

A damage threshold that changes an encounter state may trigger at most **once per player turn** unless explicitly stated otherwise.

This applies particularly to:

- Case Remand;
- Gate Height.

Multi-hit and single-hit decks therefore receive comparable access to the mechanic.

---

## 4.5 Deterministic solvability

Any generated obligation that the player is expected to fulfill must be deterministically achievable from the currently known state.

The game may never require:

- a card that is not currently available;
- an unknown future draw;
- an impossible Energy payment;
- an impossible card-type sequence.

Failure remains legal. Impossibility does not.

---

## 4.6 No infinite punishment loops

No elite-local mechanic may recursively create itself.

Scaling systems must either:

- have an explicit cap;
- be consumed;
- possess a cooldown;
- or function as intentional anti-stall escalation rather than a self-triggering loop.

## 1. The Three Appointments

**Structure:** three-body elite  
**Role:** parallel deadline / target-priority test  
**Base combined HP:** **86**

### Character / visual identity

Three increasingly imposing magical appointments have physically manifested.

- **First Appointment** is hurried and half-materialized, surrounded by small bells and loose summons.
- **Second Appointment** is heavier, tightly bound in sealed rolls and scheduled notices.
- **Final Appointment** is fully formed and eerily patient, already dressed for a meeting the player has not yet survived long enough to attend.

The encounter should feel like the calendar itself has arrived to enforce attendance.

---

### Shared mechanic — Appointment Due X

Each Appointment begins combat with its own visible source-bound countdown.

At the end of each player turn:

> Appointment Due −1.

At 0:

> that Appointment resolves its defined expiry consequence.

If the source dies:

> its Appointment Due is removed immediately.

After an expiry, that enemy receives no active countdown until it uses its scheduling move again.

### Anti-spike rule

Across the entire encounter:

> at most one Appointment may use a countdown-accelerating move in a single enemy round.

This prevents accidental triple countdown collapse.

---

### First Appointment — 24 HP

Starts with:

> **Appointment Due 2**

**Called Too Early**  
Deal **6 damage**. Apply **1 Doubt**.

**Please Wait Here**  
Gain **7 Block**.

**Move Up the Date**  
Deal **4 damage**. Reduce its active Appointment Due by 1.  
If no countdown exists, establish **Appointment Due 2** instead.

#### Expiry — First Hearing
Deal **7 damage**. Apply **1 Fatigue**.

---

### Second Appointment — 28 HP

Starts with:

> **Appointment Due 3**

**Rescheduled Cut**  
Deal **8 damage**.

**All Slots Delayed**  
Apply **1 Fatigue**.  
All living Appointments gain **4 Block**.

**Move Up the Review**  
Deal **5 damage**. Reduce its active Appointment Due by 1.  
If no countdown exists, establish **Appointment Due 3** instead.

#### Expiry — Review Convenes
Apply **2 Paperwork** and **1 Fatigue**.

---

### Final Appointment — 34 HP

Starts with:

> **Appointment Due 4**

**Appointment Strike**  
Deal **10 damage**.

**Closed-Door Pause**  
Gain **10 Block**.

**Final Reminder**  
Deal **6 damage**. Apply **1 Fatigue**. Reduce its active Appointment Due by 1.  
If no countdown exists, establish **Appointment Due 4** instead.

#### Expiry — Final Disposition
Deal **15 damage**. Apply **1 Fatigue**.

---

### Counterplay

The player is not expected to prevent every deadline.

The core decision is:

> Which expiry is acceptable, and which body must be removed before its appointment becomes due?

Killing a body reduces both action economy and future timer pressure.

## 2. Reopening-Hours Monolith

**Enemy:** Reopening-Hours Monolith  
**HP:** **92**  
**Role:** delayed damage processing / visible temporal window

### Character / visual identity

A bureaucratic building has become a single monolithic creature. Its counters, shutters and service windows open and close according to rules understood only by itself.

It does not negate the player's work.

It simply refuses to process it yet.

---

### State — OFFICE CLOSED

Combat begins:

> **OFFICE CLOSED — Reopening in 2**

While Closed, after Block is resolved:

> all HP loss caused by the player is stored as **Pending Business** instead of being removed from the Monolith's HP.

Pending Business includes player-caused HP loss originating from:

- Attacks;
- skills or powers;
- player statuses;
- relic-like effects;
- player-applied Paperwork or equivalent effects.

Pending Business is not itself damage.

### Reopening

The Closed timer ticks at the end of each player turn.

After two Closed player windows:

> the next player window is **OFFICE OPEN**.

At the beginning of that Open player window:

1. resolve all Pending Business as direct HP Loss;
2. Pending Business → 0;
3. if this kills the Monolith, combat ends immediately.

During OFFICE OPEN:

> player-caused damage resolves normally.

After the following enemy action:

> return to OFFICE CLOSED — Reopening in 2.

The entire schedule is visible.

---

### Closed-state intents

**Public Hours Suspended**  
Gain **13 Block**.

**Close Without Warning**  
Deal **9 damage**. Apply **1 Fatigue**.

**Backlog Accumulates**  
Deal **7 damage**. Gain **7 Block**.

---

### Open-state intents

**Counter Briefly Available**  
Deal **13 damage**.

**The Clerk Finally Sees You**  
Deal **8 damage**. Apply **1 Doubt**.

---

### Counterplay

Big-hit, multi-hit and indirect-damage decks are treated equivalently.

The question is not whether damage is lost.

It is:

> How much damage can I safely queue before the office agrees to process it?

A player who has already accumulated lethal Pending Business should not be forced through additional HP.

**Enemies:** Devouring Waiting Room + Minute-Moth Cloud

> **Lost Time 3**

> Lost Time is removed.

> existing Lost Time remains.

## 4. Appellate Staircase

**Structure:** three-body elite  
**Enemies:** Lower, Middle and Upper Appellate Step  
**Base combined HP:** **90**  
**Role:** procedural position / shortening-timeline tradeoff

### Character / visual identity

The appeal is a living staircase.

Each appellate level is literally a higher stone authority. A luminous Case File climbs the steps toward a final ruling.

Destroying the court structure reduces enemy actions.

It also shortens the distance to judgment.

---

### Lower Appellate Step — 24 HP

**First Ground**  
Deal **6 damage**. Apply **1 Doubt**.

**Procedural Step**  
Gain **7 Block**.

**Stone-Step Cut**  
Deal **8 damage**.

---

### Middle Appellate Step — 30 HP

**Higher Filing**  
Deal **7 damage**. Apply **1 Paperwork**.

**Procedural Landing**  
All living Steps gain **3 Block**.

**Staircase Strike**  
Deal **10 damage**.

---

### Upper Appellate Step — 36 HP

**High Objection**  
Deal **8 damage**. Apply **1 Doubt**.

**Authority Above**  
Gain **10 Block**.

**Final Step Falls**  
Deal **12 damage**.

---

### Shared resource — The Case

At combat start:

> the Case is held by the **Lower Appellate Step**.

The current Case-holder deals:

> **+2 direct attack damage**.

The Case position is always visible.

---

### Remand threshold

If the player deals at least:

> **12 actual HP damage**

to the current Case-holder during a player turn:

> Remand triggers once.

Maximum:

> **one Remand per player turn**.

If a lower living Step exists:

> move the Case down one living Step.

If the Case is already on the lowest living Step:

> apply **Stay Granted**; the Case does not automatically ascend this round.

A successful Remand also prevents automatic ascent during that round.

---

### Automatic ascent

If no Remand occurred:

> after the enemy round, move the Case up one living Step.

If it would move above the highest living Step:

> announce **FINAL RULING**.

The highest living Step becomes the Ruling source.

---

### Final Ruling reaction window

The player receives one full response turn.

Final Ruling is cancelled if, during that turn:

- the Ruling source is downed; or
- the 12-HP Remand threshold is reached against the Ruling source.

If not cancelled:

### Final Ruling
Deal **18 damage**. Apply **2 Paperwork**.

During that enemy round, all other living Steps use:

**Await the Ruling**  
The Ruling source gains **4 Block**.

They do not make their normal offensive actions.

After Final Ruling:

> move the Case to the lowest living Step.

---

### Death resolution

If the current Case-holder dies:

1. resolve its death completely;
2. cancel any Final Ruling sourced by it;
3. the killing damage cannot also create a Remand;
4. move the Case to the nearest valid living Step according to the remaining court structure.

---

### Counterplay

Killing Steps reduces action economy but compresses the appellate ladder.

The player continuously chooses between:

> fewer enemies now

and

> a faster path to Final Ruling.

## 5. Living Petition Chorus

**Enemy:** Living Petition Chorus  
**HP:** **90**  
**Role:** voluntary contract / deferred liability

### Character / visual identity

A many-voiced petition reads its own clauses aloud and repeatedly asks the player to sign.

The document grows more complete as the player accepts convenient temporary relief.

The elite is not primarily a card-count test.

It is a consent-and-consequence test.

---

### Clause offer

At the beginning of each eligible player turn:

> present one visible Clause.

A Clause may only be offered if its immediate benefit can resolve meaningfully in the current state.

Within one Reading cycle:

> each Clause appears at most once before the Clause pool refreshes.

The player chooses:

> **SIGN** or **REFUSE**.

All signed Clauses remain visibly listed with both their immediate benefit and later Liability.

Maximum:

> **3 Signatures**.

---

### Extension Clause

**SIGN**  
Gain **1 Energy** this turn.

**Liability**  
When read: apply **1 Fatigue**.

**REFUSE**  
Petition gains **8 Block**.

---

### Protective Clause

**SIGN**  
Gain **10 Block** immediately.

**Liability**  
When read: apply **2 Paperwork**.

**REFUSE**  
The Petition's next direct attack gains **+3 damage**, then this bonus disappears.

---

### Evidentiary Clause

Draw **2 cards**.

**Liability**  
When read: apply **1 Doubt + 1 Paperwork**.

**REFUSE**  
Petition gains **1 Strength**.

---

### Signature threshold

> the Petition's next intent becomes **READ INTO THE RECORD**.

The player sees:

- all signed Clauses;
- all Liabilities;
- the upcoming Read intent.

### Signature — Read Into the Record

Deal **8 damage**.

Then resolve all stored Liabilities:

> in the order the Clauses were signed.

After resolution:

- Signatures → 0;
- stored Clauses → cleared;
- begin a new Clause cycle.

## 7. Iron Warrant Avatar

**Enemy:** Iron Warrant Avatar  
**HP:** **94**  
**Role:** generated behavioral order / voluntary noncompliance

### Character / visual identity

A warrant has become a body of iron and sealwax.

It does not merely attack.

It issues a lawful demand at the beginning of the player's turn and records refusal as Contempt.

---

### Compliance Order

After normal draw:

> generate one deterministically achievable Compliance Order from the known current state.

The order must be:

- achievable;
- visible;
- non-random after selection;
- meaningfully turn-shaping where possible;
- never dependent on an unknown draw.

The same Order may not appear twice consecutively.

Initial Order pool:

**Present a Defense**  
Gain Block before first causing HP damage this turn.

**Pay the Fee**  
Spend at least **3 Energy** this turn.

**File Two Kinds**  
Play at least **two different non-Junk card types** this turn.

**Observe the Sequence**  
Play a Skill before the first Attack this turn.

Only eligible Orders enter the selection pool.

If no interesting Order is eligible:

> use a simpler valid Order rather than an impossible one.

---

### Compliance success — Compliance Credit 5

On successful fulfillment:

> gain **Compliance Credit 5** against the Avatar.

Resolve:

1. remove up to 5 Block from the Avatar;
2. any unused Credit becomes direct HP Loss;
3. this overflow HP Loss is not a Damage event.

---

### Compliance failure — Contempt

Failure creates:

> **1 Contempt**, maximum **3**.

The Avatar's next direct attack gains:

> **+4 damage per Contempt**.

After that attack:

> Contempt → 0.

If Contempt exists:

> the Avatar must select an attack on its next action and may not bank Contempt through defensive intents.

---

### Intents

**Warrant Ignites**  
Deal **11 damage**. Gain **1 Strength**.

**Iron Authority**  
Gain **15 Block**.

**Served in Steel**  
Deal **9 damage**. Gain **9 Block**.

**Binding Order**  
Gain **1 Strength + 10 Block**.  
Cooldown: **3 intents**.

**Final Service**  
Deal **17 damage**.  
Earliest: **enemy round 3**.

---

### Counterplay

Compliance is beneficial, not mandatory.

The player may intentionally reject an inefficient order and accept a known future enforcement cost.

## 8. The Seizure Procession

**Structure:** three-body elite  
**Enemies:** Inventory Lantern + Lock Cart + Seizure Marshal  
**Base combined HP:** **96**  
**Role:** temporary deck access / process-machine target priority

### Character / visual identity

The encounter is a moving confiscation procedure.

- the Lantern identifies property;
- the Cart removes access to it;
- the Marshal converts successful seizures into force.

Each body represents a different procedural step.

---

### Inventory Lantern — 24 HP

> mark one eligible card in the current hand as **Inventoried**.

Eligible means:

- non-Status;
- non-Junk;
- not already Inventoried;
- not already Seized;
- legal to play under normal card rules.

The card may cost more Energy than the player currently has remaining.

If no eligible card exists:

> no Inventoried mark is created.

If the Cart is full:

> no Inventoried mark is created.

If the marked card leaves the hand for any reason other than Seizure:

> remove Inventoried.

If the marked card is played:

> resolve it normally, then remove Inventoried.

### Lock Cart — 32 HP

> **2 Seized cards**.

# 6. Final Pool Audit

## 6.1 Mechanical redundancy

The ten encounters occupy distinct primary mechanical spaces:

- parallel countdowns;
- delayed damage processing;
- Energy-to-threat conversion;
- positional procedural escalation;
- voluntary contract debt;
- alternative death / kill-order routes;
- generated behavioral obligations;
- temporary card seizure;
- player-controlled combat stance;
- deadline plus explicit mitigation choice.

The pool no longer relies heavily on the standard Act-I card-count / card-type-repetition space.

The following former elite mechanics are deliberately removed:

- old **Office Closed** 5-damage-per-hit cap;
- old **Binding Precedent** card-type repetition rule;
- old card-play-count **Signatures** rule;
- old **Lockdown** card-count-to-Block rule.

They are superseded by the final mechanics in this document.

---

## 6.2 Archetype fairness

### Big-hit decks
No longer punished by the Reopening-Hours Monolith. Pending Business stores actual HP loss after Block regardless of hit size.

### Multi-hit decks
Do not gain multiple Remands or Gate rises in one turn.

### Skill / Power-heavy decks
Are not structurally punished by the Petition.

### Defensive / scaling decks
Can solve deadlines and procedural mechanics through timing, target priority and mitigation, but intentional stalling allows normal Strength or signature escalation to become dangerous.

### Paperwork-focused decks
Are not subjected to a new elite-wide anti-Paperwork system. Act I already contains its normal Bookworm counterspace; elites do not add a second blanket invalidation layer.

### Discard / hand-control decks
Receive legitimate counterplay against Inventoried by moving the marked card out of hand.

No supported archetype is automatically invalidated by one elite.

---

## 6.3 High-roll / low-roll risks

The following points require focused playtesting:

### Three Appointments
Three-body action economy can produce unusually high incoming pressure if countdown accelerations align. The global one-acceleration-per-enemy-round rule is mandatory.

### Waiting Room
Lost Time should punish inefficient Energy use without converting involuntary Energy loss into a double penalty.

### Petition
Clause benefits must remain attractive but not mathematically dominant. Offer generation may filter out completely useless immediate benefits, but should not optimize the offer against the player.

### Iron Warrant
Order generation must prioritize meaningful eligible Orders without ever producing impossibility.

### Seizure Procession
Three-body action economy plus card access loss is one of the hardest Act-I elites. Its 96 combined HP should not be raised before playtesting.

### Portcullis
The 12-HP Gate threshold depends on actual Act-I deck damage after several rewards. Test 10 / 12 / 14 as likely tuning points, but preserve the mechanic.

### Final Notice
The player must always receive one response window after Notice reaches 0, including when Authorized Pierce causes the transition.

---

## 6.4 Softlock audit

No intentional hardlock exists.

Required safeguards:

- Office damage is delayed, never permanently rejected.
- Compliance Orders are generated only from currently achievable states.
- Petition signing is always optional.
- Remand may occur only once.
- Seized capacity is capped at two cards.
- Cart death restores all Seized cards.
- Final Ruling and Enforce both provide a reaction window.
- Gate Height cannot change repeatedly in a single turn.
- local resources clean up when their owning encounter source disappears.

---

## 6.5 Encounter duration

No elite should continue for several additional rounds after the player has clearly solved its mechanic.

In particular:

- lethal Pending Business kills immediately on reopening;
- Seizure does not receive extra HP simply because it has three bodies;
- Portcullis does not gain permanent defensive scaling;
- Three Appointments lose both action economy and deadline pressure as bodies die;
- The Remanded Case receives only one Remand;
- Iron Warrant consumes Contempt on the next attack.

# 4. Shared Act-II Elite Rules

These rules are binding unless an encounter explicitly overrides them.

---

## 4.1 Act-wide vocabulary remains limited

The only Act-II-wide core mechanics are:

- **Overdue**
- **Misfiled**
- **Referenced**
- **Redacted**

Encounter-local resources such as:

- Late Fee;
- Compression;
- Catalogue Entries;
- Echo;
- Black Ink;
- Concordance state;
- Depth Pressure;
- Past/Future Records;

exist only inside their encounter.

They do not become new Act-wide status systems.

---

## 4.2 Source binding

Overdue, References and other local obligations must remember their source whenever source identity matters.

Examples:

- Bell-issued Overdue;
- Obituary-issued Overdue;
- Causes-issued References;
- Catalogue-issued References.

Death of a source removes source-bound obligations unless the encounter explicitly states otherwise.

---

## 4.3 References are assigned after normal draw

Whenever an elite selects a current hand card for a Reference or other hand-targeted obligation:

1. normal draw resolves;
2. Misfiled replacement logic resolves;
3. the current hand becomes known;
4. eligible target selection occurs;
5. the new obligation is previewed.

The player receives a complete response window.

---

## 4.4 Card-instance cleanup

If an encounter temporarily stores, marks or moves a specific card instance:

> encounter-local card cleanup resolves before ordinary combat-end zone cleanup.

No card may disappear because an elite died while holding or tracking it.

---

## 4.5 Non-recursive secondary effects

The following categories do not recursively trigger themselves:

- direct HP Loss from elite-local rules;
- Clock Echoes;
- Perfect Silence HP Loss;
- fulfilled-Concordance HP Loss;
- Catalogue citation HP Loss;
- temporary Drawer bonuses.

A secondary delayed effect is not automatically treated as a new primary card effect.

---

## 4.6 No impossible generated puzzle

Riddles, Words, Names and References generated from the player's current state must use deterministically valid targets.

The game may never require:

- an unavailable card type;
- an unknown future draw;
- an impossible payment;
- a card that cannot legally be played;
- or hidden information that cannot be safely revealed.

---

## 4.7 Major signatures are telegraphed

Shelf Collapse, Devour the Unstated Answer, Drawer Slams Shut, major Clock distortions and Close the Record must provide at least one full player response window.

No tenth elite is required.

Act III already requires at least **3 elite encounters on every valid path**, so a nine-encounter pool gives strong run variance without padding the roster.

# 5. Shared Act-III Elite Rules

## 5.1 Universal vocabulary

No elite creates a fifth reusable Act-wide mechanic. The universal systems remain:

- **Safe-Conduct**
- **Trespass**
- **Claim**
- **Wergild**

Local resources exist only inside their encounter.

## 5.2 Safe-Conduct

Spend 1 Safe-Conduct to prevent one complete Trespass application from a concrete source.

If a source attempts 2 Trespass at once, one Safe-Conduct prevents the full application.

## 5.3 Trespass and Claim

At **3 Trespass from the same source**, remove those 3 Trespass and create **1 newly created Claim on that source**.

Claim maximum remains **3 per enemy source**.

A transfer is never Claim creation.

## 5.4 Wergild

Elite Wergild uses the normal Act-III `Make Amends` system.

Unless an encounter explicitly changes a payment route, each point may be paid with 1 Energy or one eligible Offering.

Full payment retains the canonical Safe-Conduct reward unless explicitly stated.

## 5.5 Telegraphing

If a local counter queues a signature, finish the triggering player action and then visibly replace the next enemy action. Never interrupt the player turn.

## 5.6 Source death

On source death, source-bound Trespass, open Wergild and local resources clear unless an encounter explicitly transfers them through a visible death rule.

## 5.7 Non-recursion

Direct HP Loss from elite-local settlement/counterplay is not card damage and does not recursively retrigger itself.

# 6. The Stag of Pre-Approved Violence

**HP:** **138**  
**Role:** aggressive Safe-Conduct dilemma  
**Difficulty:** Medium–High

A vast stag carries old road signs, boundary stakes and carved permission tokens in its antlers.

## Local Law — Violence Requires Leave

The first Attack card played each player turn attempts:

> **1 Trespass from the Stag**

Maximum once per player turn. The Attack still resolves normally.

## Pre-Approved Response

At the beginning of each player turn, if below the normal Safe-Conduct cap:

> gain **1 Safe-Conduct from the Stag**.

Whenever Safe-Conduct prevents Stag Trespass:

> Stag gains **1 Sanction**.

Maximum **3**.

At 3 Sanctions, finish the current player action and queue **Authorized Charge** as the Stag's next visible action.

## A Clean Fight

Whenever Stag Wergild is fully paid:

- normal Safe-Conduct reward occurs;
- Stag loses **7 HP**;
- remove **1 Sanction**.

The HP Loss is not Damage.

## Intents

**Stamp the Hoof** — **15 damage**.

**Grant Leave to Resist** — player +1 Safe-Conduct; Stag +**10 Block**.

**Antlered Enforcement** — **18 damage**; with at least 1 Claim also create **Wergild 1**. Cooldown 2.

**Mark the Verge** — gain **16 Block**; next Stag Trespass application attempts 2 instead of 1.

### Authorized Charge

> **22 damage +4 per Claim consumed**, up to 2 Claims.

Maximum **30 damage**.

Then Sanctions → 0.

The signature cannot re-queue until at least 2 ordinary Stag actions occurred.

## Counterplay

The player can spend protection and accept Sanction, take some Trespass deliberately, settle Wergild to bleed Sanction/HP, or keep Claims low before the Charge.

# 7. Grandmother Web

**HP:** **154**  
**Role:** visible customary laws / selective suppression  
**Difficulty:** High

An ancient spider occupies a white-thorn hedge. Its web holds promises, right-of-way knots and old household customs.

## Three Courtesy Threads

Three Threads are visible. Two begin **Taut**, one **Slack**.

Only Taut Threads create Trespass.

### Thread of Entry
First non-Junk card is an Attack:

> 1 Trespass.

### Thread of Measure
First consecutive equal-base-cost pair:

> 1 Trespass.

Maximum once per turn.

### Thread of Departure
End turn with no valid non-Junk card in hand:

> 1 Trespass.

## Cut the Thread

Whenever Safe-Conduct prevents Trespass from a specific Thread:

> that Thread becomes **Cut** for the rest of the current turn and the next full player turn.

When it mends:

> Grandmother Web gains **7 Block**.

## Knot the Claim

Whenever Web gains a newly created Claim:

> one Taut, non-Cut Thread becomes **Knotted**.

A Knotted Thread attempts **2 Trespass** instead of 1.

If Safe-Conduct prevents its Trespass:

> remove the Knot and Cut the Thread.

Maximum one Knot per Thread.

## Thread rotation

After every second Grandmother Web action:

> one Taut uncut Thread becomes Slack and the Slack Thread becomes Taut.

Next rotation is visible.

## Intents

**Needle-Leg Courtesy** — **8 damage twice**.

**Mend the Household Law** — mend one Cut Thread immediately and gain **14 Block**; if none Cut, gain 20 Block. Cooldown 3.

**Knot an Older Promise** — Knot an eligible Thread +8 Block; if none, deal 16 instead.

**Hospitality Has Its Price** — consume 1 Claim → **Wergild 2**.

**Close the Web Around the Guest** — **18 +4 per Knotted Thread**, max **26**. Cooldown 2.

## Counterplay

Safe-Conduct temporarily removes actual rules from the web. The player chooses which custom is worth cutting and which can remain active.

# 10. Ant Queen of the Proper Line

**Queen HP:** **160**  
**Line-Bearers:** **27 HP each**  
**Role:** formation / target order / bounded reconstruction  
**Difficulty:** High

The Queen barely moves. Three Line-Bearers carry white strips of bark in a perfectly measured procession.

## Local Law — Do Not Break the Line

When the player directly targets a Line-Bearer while a lower-numbered living Bearer exists:

> Queen attempts **1 Trespass**.

Maximum once per player turn.

AoE without a specific Bearer target does not trigger the law.

## Permitted Exception

If Safe-Conduct prevents this out-of-order Trespass:

> the targeted Bearer gains **Permitted Exception** for the rest of the player turn.

The first direct card hit against it after prevention ignores up to **6 Block**.

## Claims belong to the Queen

Bearers never hold Claims.

Any newly created Claim arising from formation Trespass:

> is created on the Queen.

## Claim thresholds

**1 Claim — Tightened Line:** acting Bearer's direct attack +3 damage.

**2 Claims — Closed Formation:** each living Bearer gains 4 Block at end of enemy turn.

**3 Claims — Royal Enforcement:** Queen may use Royal Enforcement.

## Action-density rule

Each enemy turn:

- Queen acts once;
- only the **frontmost living Line-Bearer** acts.

The other Bearers remain positional bodies.

## Bearer intents

**Carry Forward** — 9 damage; 12 at Queen 1+ Claim.

**Hold the Line** — Queen +7 Block.

**Bite Out of Order** — if previous position is dead, 13 damage; otherwise Carry Forward.

## Queen intents

**Count the Proper Order** — **12 +4 per living Bearer**, max 24.

**Replace the Fallen** — requires a missing Bearer, 1 Queen Claim and a Reconstruction Charge. Consume 1 Claim; respawn the lowest-numbered missing Bearer at **18 HP**.

Maximum Reconstruction Charges:

> **2 per combat**

**Collect the Queen's Claim** — consume 1 Claim → Wergild 2.

**Royal Survey of the Line** — gain 14 Block; close one formation gap if possible.

### Royal Enforcement

Requires 3 Claims.

> Deal **27 damage**; consume all 3 Claims; create **Wergild 2**.

If a Reconstruction Charge remains and a Bearer is missing:

> respawn only the lowest-numbered missing Bearer at 18 HP and consume one charge.

No full free reconstruction.

## Cleanup

Queen death:

> all surviving Bearers collapse and combat ends.

Bearer death closes the formation for targeting, while original position labels remain visible for reconstruction.

# 11. Juniper Injunction

**HP:** **188**  
**Role:** Claims restrict legal remedies  
**Difficulty:** High

A huge juniper hedge has grown around an old path. Bone tags from earlier travelers hang in the branches.

## Granted Use

At the beginning of each player turn:

> present **2 currently achievable Granted Uses**.

Possible categories:

- Attack
- Skill
- Base Cost 0–1
- Base Cost 2+

The player chooses one.

The first non-Junk card that turn outside the chosen Granted Use:

> attempts **1 Trespass**.

Maximum once per player turn.

No impossible category may be offered.

## Injunctions

Whenever Juniper gains a newly created Claim:

> prepare one **Injunction** against an available remedy.

Possible Injunctions:

### Against Safe Passage
Safe-Conduct cannot be spent against Juniper's **next** Trespass application. Then the Injunction expires.

### Against Coin
Juniper Wergild cannot be paid with Energy. Offerings remain legal.

### Against Offering
Juniper Wergild cannot be paid with discarded cards. Energy remains legal.

Hard safety rule:

> Juniper may never simultaneously enjoin both Wergild payment routes.

Maximum active Injunctions:

> **2**

The intended Injunction is visible before activation.

## Petition for Relief

Whenever Juniper Wergild is fully paid:

1. normal Safe-Conduct reward;
2. player removes one active Injunction;
3. remove one Juniper Claim if any;
4. Juniper loses **6 HP**.

The HP Loss is not Damage.

## Intents

**Narrow the Granted Use** — next turn only one achievable Granted Use is offered; gain 14 Block. Cooldown 3.

**Bind the Path** — 17 damage; if an Injunction is active, create Wergild 1.

**Thorned Enforcement** — consume 1 Claim; deal 20 damage; prepare one legal Injunction.

**Demand Relief in Proper Form** — create Wergild 2; gain 10 Block.

**No Remedy Is Absolute** — 15 damage; with 2 active Injunctions +7, max 22.

### Final Injunction

Requires 3 Claims.

Consume all 3 Claims.

Prepare:

- one Against Safe Passage;
- one payment-route Injunction that cannot hardlock payment.

Then:

> create **Wergild 3**  
> gain **16 Block**

No direct damage.

Always telegraphed.

Third non-Junk card in the turn:

> 1 Trespass.

First consecutive equal-base-cost pair:

> 1 Trespass.

End turn with 0 Energy:

> 1 Trespass.

# 22. Canonical Verdict

> permission → custom → crossing → restitution → formation → injunction → obsolete right → appeal → judgment.

No fifth Act-wide mechanic is introduced.

The elite layer demonstrates that the four Green-Docket systems can mean radically different things depending on who owns the Claim, who remembers the rule, whether a right is old or current, whether restitution has been accepted, and whether protection itself creates political standing.

> **Act-III standards show that law is remembered socially.**  
> **Act-III elites show that remembered law can be negotiated, cut, appealed, enjoined and weaponized.**

**Act III Elites are content-finalized pending implementation playtesting.**

# 1. Final Audit Verdict

Act IV originally contained **16 approved elite encounters**. That roster was designed against an older standard pool of more than one hundred unique enemies.

The later curated Act-IV standard redesign reduced standard combat to:

> **35 unique identities / 55 encounter templates**

and made the surviving standards substantially stronger mechanically.

The elite pool is therefore deliberately pruned to:

> **10 final encounters**

rather than preserving sixteen encounters whose questions are now partially taught by standard enemies or claimed more strongly by the Act-IV boss pool.

Act IV requires at least **4 elite encounters on every valid route**, so ten elites still provide strong run variance.

# 6. Shared Act-IV Elite Rules

## 6.1 Universal vocabulary

The only Act-IV-wide new statuses are:

- Weighed
- Burdened
- Inscribed
- Entombed
- Embalmed

Elite-only counters such as:

- Boundary Error
- Granary Seals
- Labor Owed
- Glyphs
- Wrapping
- Treasury Credit
- Answers
- Tomb-Preserved
- Watch state

are encounter-local.

## 6.2 Solvability

Any elite-generated exact requirement must be checked against the deterministic current state.

A Weighed value greater than the player's realistically spendable Energy this turn is not offered as the only option.

## 6.3 Entombed

Entombed retains its universal stun at 5.

A queued elite action does not resolve during the skipped player turn unless its own countdown explicitly says so.

## 6.4 Inscribed / Embalmed

Inscribed amplification and Embalmed decay prevention use the universal Act-IV trigger ordering.

An elite-local reaction observes the completed amplification/preservation event and never recursively creates another identical trigger.

## 6.5 Telegraphing

Major elite signatures are queued after the triggering resolution and become immediately visible.

They never interrupt a card or a Weighed end-of-turn resolution.

# 7. Surveyor of the Errant Cord

**HP:** **248**  
**Role:** player-selected precision / escalating survey dispute  
**Difficulty:** Medium–High

An ancient surveyor moves the same boundary stones and solemnly insists they were always there.

## 7.1 Choose the Survey

After normal draw, the Surveyor presents **two achievable Weighed values** selected from 1, 2 and 3.

The same pair may not repeat on consecutive player turns if another valid pair exists.

The player chooses:

### NEAR BOUNDARY
The lower offered value.

On successful Weighed:
> Surveyor gains **10 Block**.

### FAR BOUNDARY
The higher offered value.

On successful Weighed:
- Surveyor loses **10 HP**;
- remove up to **10 current Block**.

The HP Loss is not Damage.

Both choices are valid. The player chooses easier measurement versus better counterpressure.

## 7.2 Boundary Error

Failed chosen Weighed:

- player gains **1 Entombed**;
- Boundary Error +1.

Maximum:

> **2**

At 2:
> queue `The Boundary Moves`.

## Intents

**Drive the First Stake** — 16 damage + next Choose the Survey.

**Strike the Cord** — 11 damage twice.

**Disputed Ground** — 20 damage +1 Entombed.

**Re-Tension the Measure** — 22 Block; next Far-Boundary success removes 14 Block instead of 10.

### Signature — The Boundary Moves

Deal:
> **31 damage**

Apply:
> **Burdened 1**

Then:
> Boundary Error → 0.

The next offered pair must include a different Far value than the previous pair if legal.

# 18. Archetype Audit

## Burst
No elite uses a per-hit cap or permanent damage reduction.

## Multi-hit
Seal breaks and similar thresholds are capped at once per player turn.

## Defensive / Scaling
All elites carry bounded escalation, but none uses a hidden instant-death timer.

## Status builds
Living Cartouche reacts to player-side Inscribed amplification rather than enemy debuff immunity.

## Energy-heavy decks
Surveyor and Weighed encounters use achievable-value filtering.

Treasury judges actual Energy paid rather than printed costs.

## Zero-cost / card-spam decks
Treasury can classify these as Underpaid, but the player can control Quantity and use Credits. No entire archetype is disabled.

## Small hands
Processional Sphinx offers costs rather than card-count requirements.

# 22. Canonical Verdict

Act IV no longer needs sixteen elites.

The final ten cover:

> measurement → sealed stores → labor → naming → preservation → valuation → ritual choice → grave robbery → fixed time → procession.

The six removed concepts are preserved where they are strongest: inside standards or bosses.

The final pool therefore follows the same compression principle as the standard redesign:

> **fewer identities, stronger recognition, deeper mechanics.**

**Act IV Elites are content-finalized pending implementation playtesting.**
